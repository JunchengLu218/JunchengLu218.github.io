I'm a master's student in Cognitive Science in Education at Teachers College, Columbia University, and a research assistant in the Social Cognitive and Neural Sciences Lab with Dr. Jon Freeman and Postdoctoral Research Scientist Dr. Samuel A. W. Klein.

I use computational models — hierarchical Bayesian drift diffusion, MPT/Quad — to break social decisions apart into the latent processes that produce them, and to connect those processes to neural measures (EEG, fMRI). Two questions run through my work: when a group bias shows up in behavior, which component of the decision actually carries it; and how accurately people can predict their own biases.

Before Columbia I received my B.S. in Psychology from Beijing Normal University, where I worked with Dr. Xiaochun Han on the cognitive and neural mechanisms of intergroup harm.

Research

I'm interested in how social categories — race, group membership, social role — shape decisions about harm, and in what formal models can say about why. My approach is to decompose behavior into latent parameters (evidence accumulation, decision caution, automatic versus controlled processes) and then ask which of those parameters carries the effect, whether it tracks anything in the brain, and whether people have any insight into it.

Metacognitive accuracy in implicit bias prediction

Manuscript in preparation, 2026

Do people predict ingroup-affirming implicit biases more accurately than outgroup-affirming ones? Across 2,986 participant–IAT observations from 1,866 participants on four race and age IATs, I link MPT/Quad and drift-diffusion parameters to directional and continuous prediction accuracy, holding explicit attitudes constant in mixed-effects models, and test the incremental value of the process parameters through nested model comparison.

Computational and neural mechanisms of race-dependent influence of civilian suffering on collateral-harm decisions during intergroup conflicts

Juncheng Lu*, Y. Zhang*, X. Han, Z. Xiao, X. Han† (2026)

Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, revise & resubmit

Two studies (total N = 983) pairing a cross-cultural behavioral experiment with EEG. Hierarchical Bayesian DDMs and P2/N2 measures relate civilian race and pain expression to decision caution: a Race × Expression interaction appears in caution even though overt attack decisions do not differ across conditions. I led study design, implementation, and data collection with the PI, and ran all behavioral, computational, and EEG analyses.

Cognitive mechanism of killing-decision discrimination between combatants and civilians during intergroup conflict: The influence of social-role information

Y. Zhang*, Juncheng Lu*, X. Han† (2026)

Manuscript in preparation

Four studies on how social-role information shapes civilian–soldier differences in attack decisions, bystander judgments, and representations of warmth and competence. Logistic and linear mixed-effects models over participants and social roles show that the civilian–soldier difference shrinks once role information is available, across both decision and representation tasks.

Computational modeling of the weapon identification task

Independent project, Freeman Lab · Sept – Dec 2025

DDM and MPT models fit to weapon-identification behavior to examine how evidence accumulation and latent processing components contribute to biased decisions. Competing cognitive architectures were compared, and model-derived parameters related to fMRI activation to evaluate accounts of threat processing and cognitive control.
