I'm a master's student in Cognitive Science in Education at Teachers College, Columbia University, and a research assistant in the Social Cognitive and Neural Sciences Lab with Dr. Jon Freeman.

I use computational models — drift diffusion, multinomial processing trees, hierarchical Bayesian methods — to break social decisions apart into the latent cognitive processes that produce them, and to connect those processes to neural measures (fMRI, EEG). Most of my work has been about intergroup harm and implicit bias: not just whether a bias shows up in behavior, but which component of the decision carries it.

Before Columbia I received my B.S. in Psychology from Beijing Normal University, where I worked with Dr. Xiaochun Han on the cognitive and neural mechanisms of intergroup decision-making.

Research

I'm interested in how social categories — race, group membership, social role — shape decisions, and in what formal models can say about why. My approach is to decompose behavior into latent parameters (evidence accumulation rates, starting-point and response biases, automatic versus controlled processes) and then ask which of those parameters actually carries the effect, and whether it tracks anything in the brain.

Projects

Awareness of Implicit Bias and Its Cognitive Basis

Independent project, Freeman Lab · Jan 2026 – present

Fitting RT-MPT models to several Implicit Association Tests to decompose performance into the latent processes underlying implicit bias and controlled responding, then testing whether people's awareness of their own bias tracks specific model parameters — i.e. which cognitive components support metacognitive insight into biased behavior. Model specification, estimation, and comparison in R.

Computational Modeling of the Weapon Identification Task

Independent project, Freeman Lab · Sept – Dec 2025

DDM and MPT models fit to Weapon Identification Task behavior to split decision bias into latent parameters, compared across competing cognitive architectures, then integrated with fMRI activation to test model-based accounts of threat and control processes in biased decision-making.

Computational and Neural Mechanisms of Punishment Decision-Making

Undergraduate thesis, Xiaochun Han Lab · Sept 2022 – June 2025

DDM decomposition of intergroup harm-related decisions, combined with EEG to identify temporal neural markers of distinct decision stages, testing the correspondence between latent decision parameters and ERP dynamics. Contributed to design, data collection, analysis, and manuscript writing; the manuscript is now under review at JESP.
